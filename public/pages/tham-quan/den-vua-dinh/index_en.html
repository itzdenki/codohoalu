CỐ ĐÔ HOA LƯ

Temple of King Đinh

The Temple of King Đinh Tiên Hoàng was built in the 17th century on the old foundations of the former royal palace. Its architecture follows the nội công, ngoại quốc layout: an inner complex shaped like the character Công, enclosed within an outer layer shaped like the character Quốc.

The temple porch is a showcase of the carver’s art. The wooden panels (ván bưng) are decorated with themes such as dragons hunting beasts, dragons holding pearls, a pair of dragons flanking the character “Ngọc” (jade), and dragons flanking the character “Vạn” (ten thousand).

Nineteenth-century stone carving is also on vivid display on the ancient waisted column bases (cổ bồng) and the stone thresholds. Through refined openwork and relief carving, the artisans depicted many decorative themes: the “Four Sacred Creatures” (dragon, qilin, tortoise, phoenix); the “Four Seasons” (pine, chrysanthemum, bamboo, and plum, or sometimes plum, orchid, chrysanthemum, and bamboo); “Dragon Playing in Water”; “Dragon Holding the Character Thọ (Longevity)”; the “Eight Treasures”; and more. The artisans also carved famous landscapes of Hoa Lư, such as Đông Bridge, Dền Bridge, Tháp Pagoda, and Ngang Communal House.

The inner sanctuary is built in the shape of the character Công and consists of three halls: the Bái Đường (Worship Hall), the Thiêu Hương (Incense-Burning Hall), and the Chính Cung (Main Sanctuary).

The Bái Đường is dedicated to the Công Đồng (Council of Deities). Here hangs a large horizontal board inscribed “Chính thống thủy” (“The Beginning of the Legitimate Line”), praising King Đinh Tiên Hoàng as the first in Vietnamese history to open an era of independence and national unity. The board stands like a golden tablet recording the Emperor’s lasting merit for all time.

The Thiêu Hương is where the civil and military officials of the Đinh court are worshipped. Its roof frame has beautifully carved ván mê boards in the form of dragons. The dragons here are carved in scenes of hunting prey, nesting, and gathering in herds; the masterful carving shows the varied life of the dragon.

The Chính Cung houses the statues of King Đinh Tiên Hoàng, Nam Việt Vương (Prince of Nam Việt) Đinh Liễn, Crown Prince Hạng Lang, and Vệ Vương (Prince of Vệ) Đinh Toàn.

King Đinh Tiên Hoàng, whose personal name was Đinh Bộ Lĩnh, was born in the year Giáp Thân (924) in Đại Hữu village, Đại Hoàng Prefecture (now Đại Hoàng commune, Ninh Bình province). His father, Đinh Công Trứ, served as Governor of Hoan Châu (now Nghệ An). After his father died, Đinh Bộ Lĩnh returned to his home village to live with his mother, Lady Đàm Thị, tending buffaloes for his uncle every day and playing at war with reed banners. Even as a boy, he showed a talent for command. With his fellow herd boys, he used reed plumes as banners, buffaloes as horses, and stones as weapons, splitting into two sides to stage mock battles. Whenever he won, the other boys acclaimed Đinh Bộ Lĩnh as their leader, crossed their arms to form a palanquin to carry him, and walked on either side holding reed flowers, in imitation of the procession of the Son of Heaven.

The historic turning point came in 944, when Ngô Quyền died and the Ngô dynasty gradually declined. Local warlords rose up and each carved out a domain of his own, an era that history calls the Anarchy of the 12 Warlords. The urgent need was to end this fragmentation and unify the country as soon as possible, and the man who fulfilled that historic mission was Đinh Bộ Lĩnh. He recruited troops and rallied patriots to his cause, using the mountain stronghold of Hoa Lư as his initial base. Seeing that his forces were weak at first, Đinh Bộ Lĩnh formed an alliance with the warlord Trần Lãm at Bố Hải Khẩu (now in Hưng Yên province). Impressed by Bộ Lĩnh’s handsome bearing and his talent for strategy, Trần Lãm handed over his entire military command. Đinh Bộ Lĩnh brought all the forces at Bố Hải Khẩu back to merge with the righteous army at Hoa Lư, and the strength and standing of the Hoa Lư forces grew by leaps and bounds. He resolved to wage major campaigns while also persuading the warlords to submit. In his first campaign, Bộ Lĩnh used a stratagem to induce the warlord Phạm Bạch Hổ to surrender. Riding that momentum, he marched on and crushed the warlord Đỗ Cảnh Thạc, then advanced at lightning speed to defeat the warlord Nguyễn Siêu, and took the Đường Lâm citadel of the warlord Ngô Nhật Khánh as easily as turning over a hand. Victorious wherever he went, Đinh Bộ Lĩnh’s fame resounded far and wide, and his officers and soldiers hailed him as Vạn Thắng Vương (the “King of Ten Thousand Victories”).

In 968, after putting down the chaos and unifying the country, Đinh Bộ Lĩnh ascended the throne as Emperor, named the country Đại Cồ Việt, and established his capital at Hoa Lư, where he “set up the hundred officials and the six armies, and the institutions were nearly complete.” From this time on, our southern nation had its own legitimate line of rule. By proclaiming himself Emperor and establishing his own national name and reign title, Đinh Bộ Lĩnh expressed the nation’s very high sense of pride and self-reliance. That spirit was later affirmed by Lý Thường Kiệt as an undeniable truth: “Nam quốc sơn hà Nam đế cư” (“The mountains and rivers of the South belong to the Southern Emperor”). As if foreordained, the dynasties that followed (the Early Lê, Lý, Trần, Lê, and Nguyễn) never reverted to the titles of “Vương” (king) or “Tiết độ sứ” (military governor), but all styled themselves “Đế” (emperor). For this reason, historians have affirmed Đinh Bộ Lĩnh as the founder of the legitimate line and honored him as Emperor Đinh Tiên Hoàng.

To consolidate the unified, independent state, King Đinh Tiên Hoàng built the apparatus of a centralized feudal state.

In 971, the King established ranks and bestowed court robes and caps on his civil and military officials. Nguyễn Bặc was named Định Quốc Công (Duke Who Stabilizes the Nation); Đinh Điền became Ngoại Giáp; Lê Hoàn became Thập Đạo Tướng Quân (Commander of the Ten Circuits); Lưu Cơ became Đô Hộ Phủ Sĩ Cư; and Ngô Chân Lưu was granted the title Khuông Việt Đại Sư (Great Master Khuông Việt). The King’s sons were also given titles: Đinh Liễn was named Nam Việt Vương and Đinh Toàn was named Vệ Vương.

Military. To defend the state, Đinh Tiên Hoàng built a strong army of ten corps, with Lê Hoàn appointed Thập Đạo Tướng Quân. Soldiers were issued weapons and uniforms and wore the “tứ phương bình đính” cap. A strong military ensured the stability of the country.

Agriculture. The King took a keen interest in reclaiming wasteland and founding villages, expanding the area under cultivation in the coastal plains. Lữ Đường, a warlord who had submitted to Đinh Bộ Lĩnh, reclaimed land on the right bank of the Red River.

Economy. A commodity economy had taken shape within the country, along with trade with foreign lands. The state ran workshops devoted to minting coins. Đinh Tiên Hoàng had “Thái Bình hưng bảo” coins cast, the first currency in our country’s history.

Culture and the arts. Đinh Tiên Hoàng used song and performance to serve the army and lift soldiers’ morale. Lady Phạm Thị Trân, a native of Hồng Châu (now in Hưng Yên province), who taught the soldiers to sing, was granted the title Ưu Bà, and was later honored as the Founder of the art of chèo singing. Hoa Lư was thus one of the centers of court chèo. All in all, under the Đinh dynasty, the country’s economic, cultural, and social life became stable and began to flourish.

The life and career of King Đinh Tiên Hoàng affirm him as a national hero who unified the country and built the first centralized feudal state in Vietnam. This great achievement was assessed by the historian Lê Văn Hưu: “Tiên Hoàng was endowed with extraordinary talent and insight, and was the most courageous and strategic of his age. At a time when our Việt land had no sovereign and warlords each held their own domain, with a single strike all twelve warlords submitted. He then founded the nation and set up his capital, changed his title to emperor, established the hundred officials, and raised six armies, with the institutions nearly complete. It must surely have been Heaven’s will that our Việt land should once again give birth to a sage.”

In the tenth month of the year Kỷ Mão (979), the King passed away at the age of 56. After his death, the court officials placed his six-year-old son, the child-king Đinh Toàn, on the throne. But the country was then facing the threat of invasion. To protect the realm, Empress Dowager Dương Vân Nga and the army together acclaimed Lê Hoàn as Emperor, founding the Early Lê dynasty and carrying on the nation’s heroic history.
