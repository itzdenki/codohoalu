CỐ ĐÔ HOA LƯ

Dragon bed near the Outer Gate

The Long sàng was recognized as a National Treasure by decision of the Prime Minister in 2017.

The Long sàng here is not the king’s bed, but a ritual object: a platform on which offerings were prepared or arranged for presentation. It is carved in the chân quỳ dạ cá style, with kneeling-style legs curved like a fish’s belly, sturdy and imposing yet graceful. The most striking artistic feature is concentrated on the top surface of the Long sàng, where the artisan carved, in relief, a large dragon rendered with exquisite detail and an air of authority.

What makes this treasure truly one of a kind is the boldly inventive spirit of the folk artisan. Alongside the traditional sharp, pointed dragon claws, the artisans of old cleverly transformed some of them into human hands. This subtle fusion of the dragon’s sacred power with the warmth of human presence breathed life into the stone, creating a work of art that bears the mark of deep philosophy and aesthetic sensibility.

The stone Long sàng at the Outer Nghi Môn of the Temple of King Đinh is not only a unique ritual object but also a pinnacle of traditional Vietnamese stone sculpture. Gazing at this treasure, we admire all the more the talent of the artisans of old, and take fresh pride in the heroic cultural heritage of our nation.
