CỐ ĐÔ HOA LƯ

Dragon bed in Đinh Temple courtyard

This stone Long sàng dates from the 17th century and was recognized as a National Treasure by decision of the Prime Minister in 2017.

This Long sàng is not the king’s bed but a sacred ritual object, used as a place to prepare or solemnly arrange offerings for presentation.

With supreme sculptural skill, the artisans breathed life into rough blocks of stone, transforming them into a work of art that reaches an exquisite level of technical mastery. At the center of the Long sàng is a large dragon with a plump body, single-lined scales, and lance-shaped, flame-leaf curls. The dragon here symbolizes not only supreme royal authority but also carries a deep spiritual meaning: a prayer for favorable weather and abundant harvests.

Around the border of the Long sàng runs finely carved ornamentation tied to life on the water: a pair of dragons attending a spiral cloud motif, fish, and shrimp. The most unique and distinctive feature of this treasure is its humanizing stylization: interspersed among the traditional sharp claws, the artisan skillfully carved vividly lifelike human hands. To create an air of majesty, the artisan also carved two dragons in the saddle style attending on either side of the Long sàng. Together, these give it the profile of a throne with armrests, lending it solemn and reverent dignity.

Researchers regard this Long sàng as a one-of-a-kind piece and the most representative example of the art of carving ritual platforms (sập thờ) in Vietnam. Every line carved in the stone not only paints a lively picture of nature but also expresses the human aspiration for a life of abundance in which all things flourish.
