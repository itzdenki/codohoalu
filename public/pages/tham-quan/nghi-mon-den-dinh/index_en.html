Outer and Inner Gates of Đinh Temple

In traditional religious architecture, the Nghi Môn (ceremonial gate) is more than a way in and out. It marks the boundary between the outside world and the sacred space of worship. By old custom, visitors passing through a Nghi Môn, with its high threshold, should bow their heads and keep a solemn, reverent attitude. Crossing it is also regarded as a passage from the space of everyday life into a space of spiritual culture, where the nation’s cultural values are preserved.

The two gates, the Outer Nghi Môn and the Inner Nghi Môn, share a similar architecture. Each is built as a three-bay house with a three-row column structure (ba hàng chân cột). This is recognized as the earliest surviving example of three-row column framing in Vietnam, dating from the 17th century. The rafters and columns are firmly interlocked. The decorative themes are varied, but the dominant motif is the dragon. Using high-relief and openwork carving, the artisans portrayed dragons with plump bodies and lance-shaped, flame-leaf curls, carved as nesting dragons (rồng ổ) and clustered dragons (rồng đàn), expressing the wish for endless fertility and prosperity.

On the Inner Nghi Môn, besides the sacred dragon, folk artisans carved into every grain of the wood the simple yet deeply felt beauty of human life. There are vigorous scenes of men fighting beasts, conveying humanity’s power to conquer nature and its aspiration to rise; and images of animals resting quietly, evoking a peaceful, tranquil Vietnamese countryside. The human figure is especially elevated in the depictions of celestial dancers riding dragons, their bodies supple in graceful dance poses, at once majestic and approachable.

Overall, although these carvings draw on everyday life, they possess sculptural value of the highest order and carry rich symbolic meaning. The subtle blend of the solemnity of a sacred place with the warmth of ordinary life has breathed new vitality into the space, creating a distinctive architectural setting with a contemplative beauty and an aura of the sacred that is the hallmark of the Temple of King Đinh.
