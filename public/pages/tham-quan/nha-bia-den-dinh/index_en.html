CỐ ĐÔ HOA LƯ

Stele house of Đinh Temple

The system of steles at the Temple of King Đinh Tiên Hoàng is an extremely precious “stone archive of history.”

The stele house currently holds three ancient steles carved in stone and inscribed in Chinese characters (Hán).

The three steles were carved at three different times, and each bears its own historical mark:

First is the stele of the 9th year of the Hoằng Định reign (1608). This stele records the merits of Đô Nguyên Soái Tổng Quốc Chính, Thượng Phụ Bình An Vương (Grand Marshal in Charge of State Affairs, Prince of Bình An, that is, Lord Trịnh Tùng), who, out of reverence for the virtue of the sage king of an earlier dynasty, carefully allotted seven mẫu (a traditional land measure) of rice fields to support his worship, praying for the nation’s prosperity. It also acknowledges the contributions of Phong Quận Công Bùi Thời Trung and the local people in organizing the restoration of the temple.

Next is the stele of the 17th year of the Chính Hòa reign (1696), the largest of the three and the one with the richest artistic value. Remarkably, it is also the only stele that records in full the names of the people who made it. The inscription text was composed by a doctoral laureate (tiến sĩ) surnamed Nguyễn, who held the post of Hàn Lâm Viện (Imperial Academy) official; the Chinese characters were written by the abbot of Kim Cương Pagoda; and the talented craftsman who chiseled and carved it was the artisan Lê Nhân Phú, a native of An Hoạch commune, Đông Sơn district, Thanh Hóa province.

Exploring the decorative art on the stele, visitors cannot help being amazed at the marvelous fusion of solemn courtly style and free-spirited folk sensibility. The forehead of the stele is dominated by the motif of a pair of phoenixes facing the sun (lưỡng phượng chầu nhật). Around it are symmetrical floral patterns and coiling dragons, interspersed with a warm, familiar scene of a monkey family tenderly carrying one another on their backs.

Most unique of all is the motif of two mice attending a crab (lưỡng chuột chầu cua), carved in vivid relief on the stepped section of the stele base. Crabs and mice are animals bound up with the rice fields and the humble life of farmers, yet here they are given a solemn place of ornament on a stele in the halls of a royal temple. It is a combination unmatched in ancient carving art.

In contrast to the simplicity and rustic charm of the front, the back of the stele is carved with a softly undulating dragon, symbolizing royal might. Yet instead of appearing in majesty, the dragon is hidden behind the stele. It is this subtle blend of the solemn, sacred air of the royal court with the playful humor of village life that gives this stele its one-of-a-kind value.

Third is the stele of the 3rd year of the Thiệu Trị reign (1843). It records that in the 18th year of the Minh Mệnh reign (1838), when the temple had fallen into disrepair, the provincial officials Trần Văn Trung and Bùi Cung Tiên reported to the court, and the King granted 500 quan (strings of coins) for its repair. To commemorate this kindness, in the 3rd year of Thiệu Trị this stone stele was erected, to be handed down for all time.

It can be said that the system of steles at the Temple of King Đinh Tiên Hoàng is a collection of “letters” sent from the past, fully preserving the devotion and moral principles of earlier generations. Through the ups and downs of time, these treasures of stone quietly continue to nurture national pride, reminding each of us of our cultured heritage and teaching us to cherish the sacred values of our country’s independence.
