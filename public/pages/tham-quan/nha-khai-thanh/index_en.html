CỐ ĐÔ HOA LƯ

Khải Thánh Hall

Within the architectural ensemble of the Temple of King Đinh Tiên Hoàng, the Khải Thánh House is a structure of exceptional spiritual and historical significance.

It is a sacred space dedicated to Quốc Phụ (National Father) Đinh Công Trứ and Quốc Mẫu (National Mother) Lady Đàm Quý Thị, the parents who raised an outstanding hero of the nation. The presence of this structure within the worship complex forms a complete whole: it honors the great merit of the founder of the country, while also expressing deep gratitude to the forebears who laid a firm foundation for the career of the first Emperor to build the state of Đại Cồ Việt.

In architecture, the Khải Thánh House strongly bears the artistic stamp of the 19th-century Nguyễn dynasty. It is built in the traditional shape of the character Nhị (“two”), with two halls: the Tiền Bái (front worship hall) and the Hậu Cung (rear sanctum). The Tiền Bái holds the ancestral tablets, written in Chinese characters, of the National Father and National Mother, while the Hậu Cung is where their statues are enshrined.

Tracing the historical roots: National Father Đinh Công Trứ was the father of King Đinh Tiên Hoàng. The Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư (Complete Annals of Đại Việt) records: “The King’s family name was Đinh and his personal name was Bộ Lĩnh, a native of Hoa Lư, Đại Hoàng Prefecture… the son of Đinh Công Trứ, Governor of Hoan Châu. Công Trứ had earlier served as a military aide to Dương Đình Nghệ and was assigned the post of Governor of Hoan Châu; he later went over to the Ngô King, kept the same post, and then died.”

In his lifetime, Đinh Công Trứ served as a military aide under Dương Đình Nghệ, was entrusted with the post of Governor of Hoan Châu, and then continued to serve the Ngô King. That he held such important positions shows that he was a respected military officer who took part directly in the political and military turning points of the country in the 10th century. His place in the worship system of the Hoa Lư Ancient Capital therefore not only expresses gratitude to King Đinh’s father but also honors a generation of forebears who helped build the political and military foundation for the nation’s era of independence and self-rule.

Beside the heroic father, historical records also remember the debt owed to National Mother Lady Đàm Quý Thị, a woman born in Bắc Ninh province. After her husband died, she took her son Đinh Bộ Lĩnh back to the family’s home village in the Ninh Bình region to live. That decision was a turning point: it was on this land of Ninh Bình, renowned for its sacred landscape and its talented people, that Đinh Bộ Lĩnh was sheltered, nurtured, and grew to manhood.

In folk sources, Lady Đàm Thị is linked with Đinh Bộ Lĩnh’s boyhood years through tales of the boy who drilled his troops with reed banners and rallied his friends around him. These anecdotes vividly portray the intelligence and heroic spirit that showed themselves early in a wise ruler.

The image of National Mother Lady Đàm Thị thus not only distills the lofty value of sacred maternal love, but also exemplifies the vital role of Vietnamese women in building the foundation of the family, raising talented people, and helping bring about great events in the life of the nation.

Through time and the ups and downs of history, the Khải Thánh House at the Hoa Lư Ancient Capital remains a keeper of the fine traditional moral principle of “when drinking water, remember its source,” reminding today’s and future generations always to remember the great debt owed to the forebears of the nation’s earliest days of building and defending the country.
