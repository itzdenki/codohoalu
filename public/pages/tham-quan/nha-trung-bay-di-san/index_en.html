CỐ ĐÔ HOA LƯ

Đinh and Early Lê heritage exhibition

Dear guests, to give vivid evidence of the formation and brilliant development of the Hoa Lư Capital, in 1977 archaeologists carried out a large-scale excavation in this area. In the course of it, they successfully discovered a brick-paved courtyard from the floor of a palace, dating from the 10th century, in the Đinh – Early Lê period.

The results of the excavations in this area established scientifically that the central area of the ancient Hoa Lư Capital is also the center of today’s Hoa Lư Ancient Capital heritage site.

The exhibition house holds a rich collection of original artifacts, combined with scientific documents and visual art solutions, giving an overview of the appearance of the 10th-century Hoa Lư Capital, as well as the work of conserving and promoting the value of the monument in contemporary life.

Stepping into the exhibition space, visitors can admire representative artifacts of the 10th century such as jugs, large jars, bowls, and plates… and especially the collection of “Thái Bình Hưng Bảo” and “Thiên Phúc Trấn Bảo” coins, issued in the Đinh – Early Lê period, which not only affirm the nation’s consciousness of independence but also contributed to the development of the country’s economy.

A highly precious collection of capital-building materials is also displayed here, with bricks bearing the inscription “Đại Việt quốc quân thành chuyên” (“Brick of the Military Citadel of the State of Đại Việt”), decorative bricks with lotus and phoenix designs, and the cylindrical roof tiles typical of court architecture of the time.

Visitors can also see the Buddhist Sutra Pillars, created by Nam Việt Vương Đinh Khuông Liễn, eldest son of King Đinh Tiên Hoàng. These are original artifacts, one of a kind, with extremely rare characteristics within the body of Vietnamese stele inscriptions and stone-carved texts. The inscriptions on the sutra pillars provide a wealth of priceless historical information, deeply reflecting matters of religion, social relations, and the system of land grants in the Đinh period. Notably, this is the earliest dated stele inscription from Vietnam’s era of autonomous feudal rule that survives to this day. In recognition of these especially representative historical and cultural values, in 2024 the Buddhist Sutra Pillars collection was honored to be recognized as a National Treasure by decision of the Prime Minister.

The artifacts on display show us that although the decorative themes on objects of the Đinh – Early Lê period are still rustic and simple in expression, they strongly carry the stamp of national cultural identity. That artistic foundation created the basic preconditions for Vietnamese culture to rise and flourish brilliantly in later historical periods.
